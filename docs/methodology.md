# Data and model cards

## Source and permissions

CFPB is the source of the data. Its [database page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/) permits use, analysis and building on published complaint data. The [August 14, 2026 announcement](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-to-cease-discretionary-publication-of-complaint-narratives-and-visualizations/) states that previously published narratives are in the public domain for FOIA purposes. This is the source basis for analysis; no separate dataset licence is invented. Our code is MIT-licensed; third-party software and CFPB data retain their own terms. The API repository's CC0 licence describes its code and is not treated as an independent licence grant over every complaint.

On October 11, 2026 the documented API responded to a basic metadata search but a request using the former narrative filter returned 404. The current official API specification no longer lists that filter. The authoritative narrative source is now the [FOIA archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/foia-requests/foia-electronic-reading-room/cfpb-consumer-complaint-database-narratives-archive/). Five ZIP files covering January–June 2026 were downloaded sequentially with bounded timeouts and cached locally. Archive hashes, byte sizes, request URLs, columns, acquisition time and eligible row counts are in `reports/manifest.json`. Source field definitions: [CFPB reference](https://cfpb.github.io/api/ccdb/fields.html).

## Sampling and quality

Scope is receipt dates January 1–June 30, 2026. Eligibility requires a published narrative with at least 40 non-whitespace characters. Stream the official full archives in 25,000-row chunks and retain the lowest SHA-256 hashes of `42|Complaint ID`, capped at 2,000 per month. This samples eligible narratives within each month deterministically, without reading outcomes or categories for selection. The archives contain more narratives than the retained 12,000-row analytical sample. Bulk archives are necessary because the official source offers no bounded narrative API. They are ignored by Git and Docker.

The monthly sample is unweighted. All dashboard trends and issue shares describe this sample, with explicit sample denominators, rather than population counts. Equal monthly caps change the contribution of months with differing archive volume. Narrative availability and minimum length add selection bias. We do not extrapolate to all CFPB complaints or customers. Archive `rows - eligible_narratives` counts records excluded by the period/narrative filter; eligible records above each cap are sampling exclusions. Later quality exclusions reconcile separately in `reports/audit.json`.

Required receipt date, product, issue, ID and narrative are validated; optional fields have explicit missingness counts. Identifier duplicates retain the first record. Sort by date then ID and retain the earliest occurrence of normalized narrative duplicates. Normalization lowercases and retains alphabetic tokens of length at least two. For near-duplicates, form sets of five-word shingles, use seeded 128-permutation MinHash LSH with candidate threshold 0.75, then exclude candidate matches with exact Jaccard similarity ≥0.85. This applies across the whole sampled timeline before partitioning and never copies a later record into an earlier partition. It preferentially excludes repeated later templates. LSH is approximate and can miss matches; semantic paraphrase leakage remains possible. The policy removes common duplicates but is not proof that every form of leakage is absent.

Private `clean.parquet` and DuckDB retain prepared data for local verification. Only SQL aggregate tables, anonymous routing metadata, generic lexicon weights and reports are public. Public queue identifiers are test positions, not source complaint identifiers; dates, companies, geographies and narratives are omitted. Published review metadata alone cannot support human adjudication. To inspect actual errors, reacquire the sample locally and review private data.

## Metric definitions and uncertainty

`sql/metrics.sql` groups usable sampled records by receipt month, product and observed issue. `reports/evidence.csv` is that output. `insights.py` uses DuckDB to aggregate these rows. Volume ranks product/issue pairs by sample count. Share change compares January–March and April–June using each period's usable sample count as its denominator. Both signals remain visible. Change ranking requires at least 50 records; it is a sensitivity view, not a statistical discovery test. No weighted severity or customer-harm score is claimed.

Wilson intervals accompany model correctness and issue shares in machine-readable reports. They are descriptive binomial intervals; independence is doubtful where complaints share campaigns or templates. Issue change uses a seeded 1,000-resample month-block bootstrap, independently resampling the three months in each period. Three blocks per period are insufficient for strong inferential conclusions. No p-values, seasonality or causal effects are claimed. Known timely response share divides Yes by Yes+No; unknown values are excluded from that denominator and reported. It is not satisfaction or successful resolution. Receipt-to-forwarding time is not used as a resolution proxy.

## Chronological experiment

Task: predict observed product from narrative alone, in English. Observed labels are consumer-selected routing proxies, not independently adjudicated truth. Eleven categories each had at least 30 training records. Mapping and support decisions use January–March training only. The same task and full locked test denominator apply to the majority and trained baseline. Unknown/unsupported test labels map to `__UNSEEN__` and count as errors in accuracy and macro-F1. There were none in the frozen June test. Inference cannot reliably detect an unseen semantic category: high confidence is not proof of in-domain input.

Partitions after duplicate removal: January–March train; April tune; May threshold selection; June locked test. A manually reviewed generic finance/service lexicon restricts permitted model tokens so no names, account numbers or raw vocabulary are published. Within that lexicon, TF-IDF vocabulary is learned on training only with minimum document frequency 3, sublinear term frequency and L2 normalization. Document frequencies/IDF are never fitted on tuning, calibration or test data. This deliberate privacy constraint limits model quality, especially for rare categories and specialized language. No product, issue, company, response or post-submission field enters the predictors.

Logistic regression uses seed 42 and maximum 1,000 iterations. April macro-F1 selects C from 0.3, 1 and 3, favoring lower C on ties. No fitting on April, May or June follows that selection. May chooses the highest-coverage threshold among 0.35–0.95 in 0.01 steps achieving at least 90% observed correctness and at least 50 accepted cases. If none qualifies, all cases go to review. This is threshold selection, not probability calibration. Confidence remains a raw softmax score. June is evaluated only after the pipeline and threshold are frozen; later reruns reproduce it without changing model choices.

Report accuracy as correct/total, macro-F1 over the test label universe, complete class precision/recall/F1 and confusion counts, majority baseline, accepted correct/accepted and accepted/total together, unseen-label count, and month-to-month metrics. Training months are explicitly in-sample; April and May influenced selection. Only June is a locked out-of-time test, so long-term stability has not been established. Calibration met its target in May but did not guarantee June correctness. Frozen embeddings and LoRA are not implemented or claimed. They require a separately registered, new chronological test period rather than tuning against the already opened June test.

## Deployment

GitHub Pages serves the dashboard and safe model coefficients, with client-side read-only inference on four fixed synthetic examples. No visitor upload or arbitrary private-text submission is offered. The same site is served by a non-root CPU Docker/FastAPI runtime; its endpoint accepts only an enumerated synthetic example ID. Logs omit access requests; no narrative inference logs or secrets are committed. A bounded browser check verifies filters, reset, synthetic inference parity and review views at 1440×1000 and 390×844. This is a public portfolio prototype, not an enterprise deployment or measured service improvement.
