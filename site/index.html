How does the model work? Why this method?

TF-IDF turns words into numbers. Words that distinguish messages receive more weight than words appearing everywhere. The vocabulary and word frequencies are learned from the training months only.

Logistic regression learns category scores. It combines weighted words to choose one of 11 product categories. This established method gives a compact baseline that can run in a browser.

Privacy affects the design. Only 164 learned tokens from an approved generic vocabulary are exported. This keeps raw complaint vocabulary out of the public model, but limits what it can recognise.

April selects regularisation strength C from 0.3, 1 and 3 using macro-F1. C = 1 was selected. June never chooses the vocabulary, settings or review cutoff.

How the model was tested · detailed results

TF-IDF turns words into numeric features; logistic regression uses those features to choose a category. We compare predictions with the published product labels, which can themselves be imperfect.

Reading the class results: precision asks how often a suggested category was right. Recall asks how many actual cases in that category were found. F1 balances those two. Macro-F1 gives each category equal weight, so a common category cannot hide every rare-category failure.

How was the review cutoff chosen? Where does it fail?

May selected a score cutoff of 0.66: the highest coverage meeting at least 90% observed correctness with at least 50 accepted cases. This was threshold selection, not probability calibration.

On June, 815 of 907 suggestions above the cutoff matched the published category (89.9%). The remaining 903 of 1,810 messages were flagged. A high score still allows mistakes.

Rare categories remain weak: the model identified 0 of 22 prepaid-card cases and 1 of 18 debt or credit-management cases. The published categories have not been independently checked by a person.

05 / From evidence to action

What would I do next?

  1. Review the casesUnderstand a recurring issue
  2. Propose a changeAddress the verified cause
  3. Run a small pilotCompare with the current process
  4. Measure the outcomeRepeat contacts and routing errors

The study delivers investigation priorities and a tested sorting prototype. A service pilot would establish whether they improve customer outcomes.

What change would I test, and how would I measure it?

For disputed card purchases, first review case messages and service records to check whether unclear evidence requirements contribute to repeat contacts. The complaint count alone cannot confirm this.

If that explanation is supported, test clearer dispute instructions against the current process in randomly assigned eligible cases, where feasible. Define a fixed follow-up window, compare repeat-contact rates, and monitor adverse outcomes and resolution time.

For message sorting, begin with a shadow pilot: record model suggestions while staff keep control. Measure category accuracy, handoff corrections and handling time before considering automation. These pilots have not been run; no time savings or complaint reductions are claimed.

Research and ideas behind the approach

Scikit-learn's text-classification example explains turning words into features and comparing classifiers. This project uses TF-IDF and logistic regression as a compact baseline.

Wang, Zhu and Chen (June 2026 preprint) studies complaint text using earlier data for development and later data for testing. It predicts monetary relief; this project predicts a product category. Its model and scores are not this project's results.

A newer or larger model would need to beat this baseline on fresh data and justify its cost. No large language model is used for the classifier in this demo.

Where did the data come from? How was it cleaned?

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, a US government agency, publishes complaints about financial products. This study uses its historical written accounts, not a live feed.

We selected 12,000 complaints from January to June 2026 and removed 1,050 repeated or very similar entries, leaving 10,950. People choose whether to complain and publish their account. This sample cannot represent all customers.

What can the evidence establish?

Issue counts describe this selected sample. Without customer or transaction totals, they cannot measure customer failure rates, severity or causes. The categories are recorded source labels, not themes discovered by the model.

Six months cannot establish seasonality or long-term model stability. Similar messages may remain after duplicate screening. Binomial intervals and month-block bootstrap intervals are descriptive: related complaints and only three months per comparison period limit inference.
